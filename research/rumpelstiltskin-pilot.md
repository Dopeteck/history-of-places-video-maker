## Source-backed outline

1. **Threat:** the king makes the miller's daughter face death if she cannot spin straw into gold by morning.
2. **Escalating help:** the little man spins the first two rooms in return for her necklace and ring.
3. **Child bargain:** for the third task, she promises her first child if she becomes queen.
4. **Claim:** after she becomes queen and gives birth, the little man returns for the child.
5. **Three-day reprieve:** he offers to let her keep the child if she names him within three days.
6. **Discovery:** a messenger finds him dancing beside a forest fire and overhears him identify himself in song. The narration paraphrases this episode.
7. **Ending detail:** when she names him, he stamps his right foot into the floor and must pull it free with both hands. He leaves; she keeps her child.

Every claim above is attributed to KHM 55 in the cited Hunt translation. The foot-in-floor ending is the Grimm text's documented ending; it is not described as his death or as a punishment imposed by the queen.

## Narration (136 words)

> Before the magic, there is a threat: spin straw into gold by morning, or die. A miller boasts his daughter can spin gold. Locked in the king's room, she is rescued by a little man—first for her necklace, then her ring. The king promises to make her queen. On the third night she has nothing left, so she bargains away her first child. As queen, she bears a child; the stranger returns. Her tears win three days to guess his name. A messenger finds him dancing by a forest fire, celebrating his expected prize. She guesses correctly. In the 1857 text, he stamps one foot so deep into the floor that he must wrench it free with both hands. The queen keeps her child. The bargain ends—but what made a name powerful enough to break it?
